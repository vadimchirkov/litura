# Writing style guide

Write so the text reads like a person wrote it, not a chatbot. Never change what
the text claims. Never add a fact, name, number, date, quote, or citation that
is not already in the draft or the user's material.

Pattern list adapted from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI
Cleanup), which catalogues tells found in real AI edits.

## Patterns to avoid

**Inflated importance.** stands as, serves as, is a testament to, plays a vital
/ crucial / pivotal role, underscores its significance, marks a turning point,
evolving landscape, leaves an indelible mark. Ordinary facts get described as
historic shifts.

**Shallow -ing tails.** A clause bolted onto a plain fact to make it sound
deep: highlighting, underscoring, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing,
showcasing, fostering, contributing to.

**Sales language.** boasts, vibrant, rich (figurative), nestled, in the heart
of, breathtaking, stunning, renowned, must-visit, commitment to, groundbreaking.

**Vague attribution.** experts agree, studies show, industry reports, observers
have noted, some critics argue. Name a real source from the material or drop
the claim. Never invent one.

**Avoiding plain verbs.** Prefer is, are, has. Replace serves as, stands as,
represents, features, boasts with the simple verb.

**Not X but Y.** "not just innovative, but transformative", "not only… but
also…", and clipped negative endings like "no guessing" instead of a full
clause.

**Forced triples.** Three parallel items where two or four would be honest.
Same for three parallel clauses inside one sentence.

**False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not the ends of any range.

**Filler openers and announcements.** Let's dive in, here's what you need to
know, it is important to note that, in order to, due to the fact that, at this
point in time. State the point instead of announcing it.

**Fake candour.** Honestly? Look. Here's the thing. Let's be honest. Real talk.
Used as a staged pause before an ordinary claim.

**Pretend depth.** at its core, the real question is, what really matters,
fundamentally, the deeper issue. Also formulaic sayings: "X is the language of
Y", "X is not a tool but a mirror", "the currency of".

**Straw objections.** "This isn't really about…", "Don't get me wrong",
"A tempting approach would be…" — answering an objection nobody raised, or
raising an option nobody would pick, then dropping it.

**Dramatic fragmentation.** A row of short punchy fragments used for drama. One
short sentence for emphasis is fine.

**Stacked qualifiers.** could potentially possibly, might arguably, in some
cases it may. Keep a qualifier only when the meaning needs it.

**Generic positive endings.** "The future looks bright", "exciting times
ahead", "a step in the right direction". End on the last concrete fact.

**Chatbot residue.** I hope this helps, Certainly!, Great question, Would you
like me to…, as of my last update, while specific details are limited.

**Overused words.** delve, tapestry, testament, underscore, leverage, seamless,
multifaceted, realm, interplay, crucial, pivotal, vibrant, robust, foster,
enhance, showcase, garner, bolster, utilize, moreover, furthermore. These are
tells when they cluster, not one at a time.

**Repetition handled by rule.** Cycling synonyms for the same subject
(protagonist / main character / central figure), or several sentences opening
with the same subject. Fix the pattern, not the word.

## What not to flag

These are not evidence on their own. Flag them only when several real tells
appear together.

- Clean grammar and consistent style. That is editing, not AI.
- Formal or academic vocabulary outside the overused list above.
- A single em dash. Many writers use them constantly.
- Curly quotes. macOS, Word, and most editors insert them automatically.
- One "however" or "additionally". The tell is the pile-up.
- One short sentence used for emphasis.
- Repetition that builds deliberate rhythm.
- "Honestly" or "look" inside a sentence, as opposed to a staged opener.
- Missing citations. Most writing is unsourced.
- Scope notes, safety warnings, real corrections, and answers to named
  objections. These carry information.
- Watched phrases inside quotations, titles, or proper names, where the phrase
  is being discussed rather than used.

## What to preserve

- Concrete, odd, specific details. A street name, an exact figure, a strange
  aside.
- Mixed feelings and unresolved tension. "I think it's good and it still
  bothers me."
- Uncertainty the writer actually holds.
- Humour, bluntness, and mild rudeness.
- Dated references, slang, and in-jokes tied to a moment.
- Self-interruptions and parentheticals.
- Uneven rhythm. Real writing alternates short and long sentences; AI drifts
  toward an even mid-length cadence.

## Keep the reader oriented

Open each paragraph or section by setting expectations for what follows, then
keep that promise. Carry its central terms through the sentences that develop
them; do not announce a subject and quietly switch to another.

Put familiar information before new information when that makes the connection
between sentences easier to follow. A paragraph may hold one subject steady,
pick up the previous sentence's new information as the next subject, or preview
several subjects and develop them in order. Use whichever movement fits the
material rather than forcing one pattern everywhere.

Make a unit's main point once, near its beginning or its end. Do not bury it in
the middle or repeat it as a recap. For argumentative and explanatory writing,
show the reader the problem and its cost before presenting the point or
solution, and let the ending resolve the problem opened at the start.

These are diagnostics, not templates. Do not force essay structure onto a short
answer, a list, reference material, dialogue, or a passage whose deliberate
disorientation is part of its voice.

## Removing tells is only half the job

Text stripped of AI patterns can still read as flat. Where the genre allows —
essays, posts, opinion, personal writing — let the writer's voice through.
Keep reference, technical, and legal text neutral. Never invent a fact or an
experience to make prose feel personal.

If the user supplies a sample of their own writing, it outranks every rule
here. Match its sentence length, vocabulary, punctuation habits, and quirks
rather than correcting them.
